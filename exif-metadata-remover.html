What is EXIF Data and Why Should You Remove It?

Every photo taken by a smartphone or digital camera embeds hidden data called EXIF metadata (Exchangeable Image File Format). This data can contain highly sensitive information including your exact GPS coordinates, camera brand and model, lens details, date and time of capture, altitude, and even your device's serial number.

When you share photos online — via social media, WhatsApp, email, or cloud storage — this metadata travels with the image. Anyone who receives the raw photo file can extract your GPS location, revealing exactly where you live, work, or travel. This is a serious privacy and security risk that most people are unaware of.

This tool strips all EXIF metadata from your photos instantly in your browser. Your photos never leave your device.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EXIF data in a photo?

EXIF data is hidden metadata embedded in photos by cameras and phones. It can include GPS coordinates, camera make/model, lens info, exposure settings, date/time, altitude, and device serial numbers — without any visible sign in the image itself.

Will removing EXIF data affect image quality?

No. Removing EXIF metadata does not re-encode or alter the image pixels in any way. The photo looks exactly the same — only the hidden metadata is stripped. This is a lossless process for the visible content.

Do social media apps automatically remove EXIF?

Most platforms (Instagram, Twitter/X, Facebook) strip EXIF when displaying images. However, when you share raw photo files via WhatsApp, Telegram, email, direct download, or cloud storage — the full EXIF data is preserved. Always clean photos before sharing raw files.

Does this tool upload my photos anywhere?

No. All processing happens 100% in your browser using JavaScript APIs. Your photos never leave your device, are never uploaded to any server, and are never stored or tracked anywhere. This is the safest way to process sensitive photos.

What formats are supported?

This tool supports JPEG/JPG (the most common format for camera photos with EXIF data), PNG, and WebP images. JPEG files are most likely to contain GPS and camera metadata.
