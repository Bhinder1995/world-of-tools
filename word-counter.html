How It Works

The word counter uses JavaScript's string processing APIs running locally in your browser:

- Words are counted by splitting text on whitespace and punctuation boundaries.
- Characters are counted as raw string length (with and without spaces).
- Sentences are detected by ., !, and ? punctuation marks.
- Paragraphs are detected by double line breaks.
- Reading time = word count ÷ 200 (average adult reading speed in words per minute).
- Speaking time = word count ÷ 130 (average speaking speed).

No server round trips, no delays — everything updates the moment you type or paste.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How many words should a blog post be?

Most SEO experts recommend 1,500–2,500 words for ranking blog posts. Long-form content (3,000+) tends to rank well for competitive keywords.

How many characters does a tweet allow?

Twitter/X allows 280 characters per tweet. Use this word counter to stay within the limit.

How long does it take to read 1000 words?

At an average reading speed of 200 words per minute, 1,000 words takes approximately 5 minutes to read.

How many words is a 5-minute speech?

At a typical speaking pace of 130 words per minute, a 5-minute speech is approximately 650 words.

Does the word counter support multiple languages?

Yes — it works with any language that uses spaces or punctuation to separate words, including Hindi, French, Spanish, German, and more.

Does it store my text?

No. The word counter runs entirely in your browser; your text is never uploaded, saved, or shared.

Can I use it to check my meta description length?

Yes — meta descriptions should be 150–160 characters. Use the character counter to check and optimize.

Is there a maximum text length?

There is no strict limit — the tool handles typical document sizes with ease. Very large files may be limited by your browser's memory.
