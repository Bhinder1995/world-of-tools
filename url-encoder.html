What is URL Encoding?

The URL Encoder & Decoder by WorldOfTools is a free, instant, and privacy-first utility to encode special characters in URLs and text into a safe, percent-encoded format — or to decode percent-encoded strings back to human-readable text. Whether you're building an API, debugging a broken link, passing query parameters, or processing redirect URLs — this tool gives you accurate results in seconds.

URL encoding (also called percent-encoding) is defined in RFC 3986. It converts characters that are not allowed in a URL into a % followed by two hexadecimal digits representing the character's ASCII value. For example, a space becomes %20, and an ampersand & becomes %26.

Unlike other online tools, all processing happens entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Your text or URLs are never sent to any server, never stored, and never tracked.

encodeURI vs encodeURIComponent — What's the Difference?

There are two standard JavaScript functions used for URL encoding, and choosing the right one matters:

encodeURI — Used to encode a complete URL. It preserves characters that have structural meaning in a URL, such as :, /, ?, #, and &. Use this when you want to make a full URL safe to transmit without breaking its structure.

encodeURIComponent — Used to encode a URL component like a query parameter value. It encodes those structural characters too, making it safe to embed any value inside a URL parameter. Use this tab's "Component Mode" for this.

Our tool's Encode URL tab uses encodeURI, the Component Mode tab uses encodeURIComponent, giving you the right encoding for every situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is URL encoding?

URL encoding (percent-encoding) converts special characters into a % followed by two hexadecimal digits. For example, a space becomes %20. It ensures your URL is valid and can be safely transmitted over the internet per RFC 3986.

What is the difference between encodeURI and encodeURIComponent?

encodeURI encodes a full URL and preserves structural characters like /, ?, and &. encodeURIComponent encodes a single component (like a parameter value) and also encodes those structural characters. Use Component Mode for parameter values.

Why does a space become %20 in a URL?

Spaces are not allowed in URLs per the RFC 3986 specification. They are percent-encoded as %20 (hexadecimal for ASCII 32). Some older systems also use + for spaces in query strings (application/x-www-form-urlencoded format).

Can I decode a URL with %2F, %3A, or other percent codes?

Yes. Switch to the Decode tab, paste your percent-encoded string, and click Decode. The tool converts all %XX sequences back to their original characters instantly, including %2F (/) and %3A (:).

Is my data stored when I use this URL encoder?

No. All encoding and decoding runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Your input text or URLs are never sent to any server, stored in any database, or shared with anyone.

What characters don't need to be encoded in a URL?

Unreserved characters — letters (A–Z, a–z), digits (0–9), and the symbols -, _, ., and ~ — never need to be encoded in a URL. All other characters should be percent-encoded to ensure safe transmission.

Can I use this to encode non-English or Unicode characters?

Yes. The tool handles Unicode characters by converting them to their UTF-8 byte sequence and then percent-encoding each byte. For example, the emoji 🚀 encodes to %F0%9F%9A%80.
