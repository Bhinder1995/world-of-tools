EMI Formula Explained

EMI = [P × R × (1+R)^N] ÷ [(1+R)^N − 1]

Where: P = Principal loan amount | R = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) | N = Loan tenure in months

Example: For a ₹20 lakh loan at 8.5% for 20 years: R = 8.5/12/100 = 0.00708, N = 240. This gives a monthly EMI of approximately ₹17,356.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI and how does it work?

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is a fixed monthly payment made to a lender on a set date. Each EMI consists of a principal component and an interest component. In the early months, the interest portion is higher; over time, the principal portion increases — this is called the reducing balance method.

How do I reduce my EMI?

You can reduce your EMI by: (1) Negotiating a lower interest rate, (2) Increasing the loan tenure (longer repayment period), or (3) Making a larger down payment to reduce the principal. Note that increasing tenure reduces EMI but increases total interest paid.

What is the maximum loan tenure for a home loan in India?

Most Indian banks offer home loan tenures up to 30 years (360 months). The maximum tenure typically depends on the borrower's age — banks generally require the loan to be repaid before retirement age (60-65 years).

Can I prepay my home loan to save interest?

Yes. Prepayment is one of the most effective ways to reduce your total interest burden. Even paying one extra EMI per year can save several months of tenure. RBI guidelines prohibit foreclosure charges on floating-rate home loans for individual borrowers.

What is the difference between fixed and floating interest rate for EMI?

Fixed rate EMI stays constant throughout the loan tenure. Floating rate EMI changes with market interest rates (linked to RBI repo rate). Floating rates are currently more common for home loans in India and can offer savings when rates fall.
