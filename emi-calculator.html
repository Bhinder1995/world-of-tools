The Anatomy of an EMI: How It's Calculated

Every EMI payment is divided into two parts: the principal amount and the interest amount. In the early stages of a loan, a larger portion of your EMI goes toward interest. Our tool executes the reducing balance formula instantly:

EMI = [P x R x (1+R)^N] / [(1+R)^N-1]
  • P (Principal): The total loan amount borrowed.
  • R (Rate): The monthly interest rate (Annual Rate / 12 / 100).
  • N (Tenure): The total number of monthly installments.

💡 Pro Tip: The Tenure Trade-off

On a ₹50 Lakh home loan at 8.5%, increasing your tenure from 15 to 30 years lowers your EMI by nearly ₹10,000, but increases your total interest paid by over ₹35 Lakhs! Always aim for the shortest tenure your budget can afford.

The Components of Your EMI

Every EMI payment is divided into two parts: the Principal (the actual money borrowed) and the Interest (the bank's fee). In the early years of a home loan, nearly 70% of your EMI goes toward interest. Understanding this "Reducing Balance" logic is crucial for planning prepayments.

💡 Pro Tip: The Tenure Trade-off

Increasing your home loan tenure from 15 to 30 years might lower your EMI by 30%, but it often doubles the total interest you pay to the bank. Always aim for the shortest tenure your monthly budget can reasonably support.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is EMI calculated?

EMI is calculated using the formula: [P x R x (1+R)^N]/[(1+R)^N-1], where P is Principal, R is monthly interest rate, and N is the number of monthly installments. Our tool automates this complex math for you.

Does the EMI amount change over time?

In a fixed-rate loan, the EMI remains constant. However, the internal breakup changes—more interest is paid at the start, and more principal is repaid toward the end. In a floating-rate loan, the EMI may increase or decrease if the bank's base rate changes.

What is a loan amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule is a complete table showing every payment of your loan. It breaks down each EMI into interest and principal and shows the remaining balance after each payment. You can find this table below the calculator.

Can prepayments reduce my EMI?

Prepayments usually reduce the total loan tenure while keeping the EMI constant, which saves you the most money in interest. Some banks also allow you to keep the tenure same and reduce the monthly EMI instead.
