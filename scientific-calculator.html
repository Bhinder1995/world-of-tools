Scientific Calculator Functions Explained

A scientific calculator extends basic arithmetic with mathematical functions used in algebra, calculus, physics, and engineering. Here's what each category does:

Trigonometry (sin, cos, tan): Compute ratios of sides in a right triangle, essential for geometry and physics. Their inverses (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) recover angles from ratios. Switch between degrees and radians depending on your problem.

Logarithms (log, ln): log computes base-10 logarithm (common log used in pH, decibels), while ln computes natural logarithm (base e, used in calculus and exponential growth). 10ˣ and eˣ are their respective inverses.

Powers & Roots: x², x³, and xʸ compute powers. √ (square root) and ∛ (cube root) compute their inverses. These are used constantly in geometry, physics, and statistics.

Factorial (n!): Used in combinatorics and probability. 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120. Note: factorials grow extremely fast — inputs above 170 overflow to Infinity.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate sin, cos, or tan online?

Enter the angle value, then press the sin, cos, or tan button. Make sure the mode (DEG or RAD) is set correctly for your problem. Most classroom problems use degrees; physics and calculus often use radians.

What is the difference between log and ln?

log is the common logarithm (base 10), widely used in science, engineering, and everyday contexts like pH or decibels. ln is the natural logarithm (base e ≈ 2.71828), the foundation of calculus and exponential growth models.

How do I use scientific notation with this calculator?

Enter very large or very small numbers normally. Results exceeding display limits are shown in scientific notation automatically (e.g., 1.23e+15). You can also type numbers like 1.5e10 directly using your keyboard.

What do the memory buttons do?

M+ adds the current result to memory. M− subtracts from memory. MR recalls the stored memory value into the display. MC clears memory. MS stores the current display value directly. The "M" indicator lights up when memory contains a value.

Is my data private?

Completely. Every calculation runs in your browser via JavaScript. Nothing is sent to any server. Your history is stored only in the page session and disappears when you close or refresh the tab.
