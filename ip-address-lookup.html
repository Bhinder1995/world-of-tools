What Is an IP Address?

An IP (Internet Protocol) address is a unique numerical label assigned to every device connected to a network. It serves two main purposes: identifying your device and providing its network location so data can be routed correctly across the internet. When you type a website address, your IP is used to send and receive information.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is my IP address?

Your IP address is a unique number assigned to your internet connection by your ISP. This tool detects and displays it automatically.

Can I find someone's location from their IP?

IP geolocation gives an approximate city or region, not an exact address. Accuracy varies by ISP and country.

What is IPv4 vs IPv6?

IPv4 uses 32-bit addresses (e.g. 192.168.1.1). IPv6 uses 128-bit addresses to support many more devices globally.

How do I hide my IP address?

Use a VPN or Tor browser to mask your real IP with a different one.

Does my IP address change?

Most home connections have a dynamic IP that may change when you restart your router. A static IP is permanently assigned.

Is my IP address private?

Your public IP is visible to every website you visit. Your private IP (like 192.168.x.x) is only visible within your local network.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes, fully responsive. Works on all devices and browsers.

📚 About IP Geolocation

IP geolocation works by mapping IP address ranges to geographic locations using databases maintained by organizations like MaxMind and IP2Location. ISPs are assigned blocks of IP addresses by regional registries (ARIN, APNIC, RIPE, etc.), and this assignment information is used to determine country, city, and ISP.

Accuracy varies — country-level detection is ~99% accurate, city-level is ~60-80% accurate, and precise street-level location is not possible from an IP alone.
