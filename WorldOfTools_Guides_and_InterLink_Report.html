GST Calculator India — Add or Remove GST at 5%, 12%, 18% & 28% Instantly
Full Guide Content (~1,200 words)

India's Goods and Services Tax system replaced a patchwork of state levies with a unified framework in July 2017. For any business owner, freelancer, or shopper, the ability to calculate GST quickly and accurately is a non-negotiable daily skill. This guide explains exactly how to use the WorldOfTools GST Calculator, how each GST slab works, and when to apply Add GST versus Reverse GST calculations — with worked examples for every scenario.

India's Four GST Slabs — What Falls Where

5% GST applies to essential items: packed food, domestic LPG, economy hotel stays, and public transport. If you sell items in this bracket, charging customers an extra 5% on base price keeps you compliant.

12% GST covers processed foods, smartphones below ₹12,000, and most mid-range goods. This is one of the most common slabs for small manufacturers and resellers.

18% GST is the workhorse slab — it covers software, IT services, financial services, restaurants, and most professional services. The vast majority of Indian freelancers and service businesses register under this rate.

28% GST targets luxury and sin goods: cars, air conditioners, tobacco, aerated drinks, and five-star hotel stays. If you run a business in this category, this is the slab you'll use daily.

Add GST vs. Remove GST — When to Use Each Mode

This is the most misunderstood part of GST calculations. Add GST is used when you know the base (pre-tax) price and need to find the final customer-facing price. Example: A web developer charges ₹50,000 for a project. At 18% GST, the invoice total = ₹50,000 + ₹9,000 = ₹59,000.

Remove GST (Reverse Calculation) is used when you have a MRP or a GST-inclusive price and need to work backwards to find the base price and the tax component. Example: A product has MRP ₹1,180 and 18% GST is included. Base price = ₹1,180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,000. GST component = ₹180.

CGST, SGST and IGST — What's the Difference?

For intra-state transactions (seller and buyer in the same state), GST splits equally into CGST (Central GST, goes to Centre) and SGST (State GST, goes to State government). So on an ₹1,000 item at 18%: CGST = ₹90, SGST = ₹90, Total GST = ₹180.

For inter-state transactions (seller and buyer in different states), the entire tax is charged as IGST (Integrated GST) at the same combined rate. So the same ₹1,000 item sold across state lines = IGST ₹180. The WorldOfTools calculator shows both breakdowns.

GST Registration Threshold — Do You Need to Register?

As of FY 2025-26, businesses with annual turnover above ₹40 lakhs (goods) or ₹20 lakhs (services) must register for GST. In special category states (like Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand), the limit is ₹10 lakhs for services. Regardless of whether you're registered, understanding the GST component in every transaction makes you a more informed buyer and seller.

Worked Examples for Common Business Scenarios

Freelance invoice at 18%: Base fee ₹75,000 → GST = ₹13,500 → Invoice total = ₹88,500. CGST ₹6,750 + SGST ₹6,750.

Restaurant bill at 5%: Food total ₹2,000 → GST = ₹100 → Final bill = ₹2,100. (Note: Restaurants with AC charge 5% GST, not 12%.)

MRP reverse calculation at 28%: MRP ₹2,560 (GST inclusive) → Base price = ₹2,560 ÷ 1.28 = ₹2,000. GST component = ₹560.

Common GST Mistakes That Cost Businesses Money

Applying the wrong slab is more common than most people admit. Misclassifying a service at 12% instead of 18% creates underpayment liability. Conversely, charging 28% on items in the 18% bracket overcharges customers and creates refund complications. Always verify your HSN/SAC code against the official GST rate schedule before setting prices.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is 18% GST charged on all IT services in India?
A: Yes — software development, consulting, SaaS subscriptions, digital services and IT-enabled services (ITES) all fall under the 18% slab under SAC code 998314 and related codes. There are narrow exceptions for government or export services.

Q: Can I claim input tax credit (ITC) on GST paid?
A: Yes, if you are a GST-registered business, you can claim ITC on GST paid on business inputs, reducing your net tax liability. The calculator helps you identify the exact GST amount paid — which is the figure you use when filing returns for ITC claims.

Q: What is a composition scheme and does GST change?
A: Under the GST Composition Scheme, small businesses (turnover up to ₹1.5 crore for goods, ₹50 lakh for services) pay a flat tax rate of 1%–6% depending on business type. They cannot collect GST from customers or claim ITC.

Q: What is the GST on gold and jewellery?
A: Gold attracts 3% GST. Making charges on jewellery attract 5% GST. So a gold necklace worth ₹1,00,000 + ₹10,000 making charges = ₹3,000 GST on gold + ₹500 GST on making = ₹1,13,500 total.

The EMI Formula Every Bank Uses

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is calculated using this formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ [(1+r)^n - 1], where P = Principal loan amount, r = Monthly interest rate (Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n = Loan tenure in months. The formula looks complex but the WorldOfTools calculator handles it instantly — just enter three values.

Home Loan EMI Examples (2025-26 Rates)

SBI home loan rates start from approximately 8.50% p.a. for salaried borrowers with 750+ CIBIL score. Let's calculate EMI for ₹50 lakh at 8.5% for 20 years: Monthly rate = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083. n = 240 months. EMI ≈ ₹43,391/month. Total repayment over 20 years = ₹1,04,13,840. Total interest = ₹54,13,840 — over half the principal!

This is why tenure matters more than most borrowers realise. Reducing to a 15-year tenure raises EMI to ₹49,272 but saves ₹13.5 lakh in interest.

Car Loan EMI Calculations

Car loans in India typically range from 7.5%–12% p.a. with tenures of 1–7 years. For a ₹10 lakh car loan at 9% for 5 years: EMI ≈ ₹20,758/month. Total interest paid = ₹2,45,480. A key tip: a higher down payment (20–30% of car value) drastically reduces total interest outgo.

Personal Loan EMI — The Expensive Category

Personal loans carry the highest rates in the market — typically 10.5%–24% p.a. For ₹3 lakh at 14% for 3 years: EMI ≈ ₹10,247/month. Total interest = ₹68,892 — that's 23% of the principal. Personal loans should be used only for emergencies or high-ROI purposes, not for consumption.

What Is an Amortization Schedule?

An amortization schedule shows month-by-month how each EMI splits between principal and interest repayment. In the early months of a loan, most of the EMI goes toward interest — not principal. In a ₹50L home loan, the first EMI of ₹43,391 might include ₹35,417 in interest and only ₹7,974 of principal repayment. This inverts over time, with later EMIs being mostly principal. Prepayment Strategy — When to Pay Extra

Making lump-sum prepayments early in a loan's life has an outsized impact. Paying an extra ₹1 lakh in year 2 of a 20-year home loan can reduce the tenure by 2–3 years and save ₹4–8 lakh in interest. Most banks allow partial prepayment without charges on floating-rate loans (RBI directive). Always check for fixed-rate loan prepayment clauses.

How Salary Affects Loan Eligibility

Banks typically cap total EMI obligations at 40–50% of take-home salary. On ₹60,000/month take-home, the maximum EMI a bank would approve is ₹24,000–30,000. If you already have a car loan EMI of ₹8,000, the remaining eligible home loan EMI is ₹16,000–22,000, which translates to a home loan of approximately ₹18–25 lakh (depending on tenure and rate).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does CIBIL score affect my EMI?
A: CIBIL score determines the interest rate you get. A score above 750 earns the lowest available rates; below 650 may result in rejection or rates 3–4% higher, significantly increasing your EMI and total cost.

Q: What is the processing fee on Indian home loans?
A: Usually 0.25%–1% of loan amount. On a ₹50L loan, that's ₹12,500–₹50,000 upfront — a hidden cost many borrowers overlook. Factor this into total loan cost calculations.

Q: Is it better to choose floating or fixed interest?
A: In a falling rate environment, floating rates benefit borrowers as EMIs decrease automatically (for tenure-adjusted loans) when the RBI cuts the repo rate. Fixed rates offer predictability. Currently (2025), most experts suggest floating rates as the RBI cycle is expected to ease.

What Is SIP and Why It Beats Lump Sum (Usually)

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) invests a fixed amount every month into a mutual fund, purchasing NAV units at whatever price they trade that day. Over time, this rupee-cost averaging means you buy more units when prices fall and fewer when prices rise — automatically smoothing out market volatility. Lump sum investing requires perfect market timing, which even professionals fail at consistently. SIP removes the timing requirement entirely.

The SIP Calculator — What the Numbers Mean

Enter three values: monthly investment (₹), expected annual return (%), and investment duration (years). The calculator returns: total amount invested, estimated returns, and maturity value. The key insight is the compounding gap — the bigger the gap between your total invested amount and the maturity value, the harder your money is working.

  • 20 years at 14%: Invested ₹12L → Maturity ₹66.8L (+457%)
  • 30 years at 14%: Invested ₹18L → Maturity ₹3.72Cr (+1967%)

Time is the most powerful variable in this equation — not return rate. A 1% difference in return rate over 30 years changes the outcome by lakhs. Adding 5 more years doubles the outcome at the same rate. Start early, stay consistent.

SIP vs Lump Sum — When Each Wins

SIP wins when: Markets are volatile, overvalued, or you don't have a large corpus to invest. You're a salaried individual investing monthly income. You lack the discipline to time the market correctly.

Lump Sum wins when: Markets have just corrected 25%+ and valuations are clearly cheap (P/E below 16 for Nifty). You have a windfall (bonus, inheritance) that you want to deploy immediately. Historically, lump sum outperforms SIP ~60% of the time, but only when timing is right.

Choosing the Right Return Rate Assumption

The rate you plug into the SIP calculator dramatically changes the output. Use these evidence-based benchmarks: Large-cap equity funds: 10–12% CAGR (10-year historical average). Mid/small-cap funds: 13–16% CAGR. Hybrid/balanced funds: 9–11%. Debt funds: 6–8%. Index funds (Nifty 50): ~12% over 10+ years. Never use more than 12% for long-term planning unless you have specific reasons to expect higher.

Step-Up SIPs — The Game Changer

A step-up SIP increases your monthly investment by 10% every year. Starting with ₹5,000/month and stepping up 10% annually: year 1 = ₹60,000, year 5 = ₹87,978, year 10 = ₹1,41,774 annually. The maturity value over 20 years at 12% = approximately ₹1.3 crore — vs ₹49 lakh from a flat ₹5,000 SIP. This reflects income growth naturally and accelerates wealth building dramatically.

Tax on SIP Returns — LTCG and STCG in 2025

For equity mutual funds held over 1 year, gains above ₹1 lakh in a financial year attract 12.5% Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax (post-Budget 2024 revision). Gains within 1 year attract 20% Short Term Capital Gains (STCG) tax. For debt funds, gains are added to income and taxed at slab rate. SIPs redeemed after 1 year per installment qualify for LTCG treatment — each monthly installment has its own 1-year clock.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What happens to my SIP if the market crashes 40%?
A: Your portfolio value falls short-term, but you now buy significantly more units per month at cheaper prices. Historically, investors who continued SIPs during the 2008, 2020, and 2022 crashes recovered and outperformed those who stopped. Crashes are SIP opportunities, not disasters.

Q: Can I pause my SIP if I need cash?
A: Yes — most AMCs allow 1–3 month pauses without penalty. However, frequent pausing disrupts rupee-cost averaging benefits. It's better to reduce the SIP amount than stop entirely.

Q: What is ELSS and how does it differ from regular SIP?
A: ELSS (Equity Linked Saving Scheme) is a type of equity mutual fund with a mandatory 3-year lock-in per installment that qualifies for ₹1.5L annual deduction under Section 80C. Returns are comparable to large-cap equity funds. It's the most tax-efficient SIP option for most investors.

PPF Interest Rate History & Current Rate

The PPF interest rate is set by the Government of India quarterly. For Q1-Q4 FY 2025-26, the rate is 7.1% per annum, compounded annually. Historically, PPF rates ranged from 12% (in the 1980s) to a low of 7.1% seen since 2020. The rate is linked to government security yields and reviewed every quarter by the Finance Ministry.

How PPF Interest Is Calculated (The "5th of the Month" Rule)

Here's the lesser-known PPF timing rule that most investors ignore: PPF interest is calculated on the minimum balance between the 5th and last day of each month. If you deposit after the 5th, that deposit earns zero interest for that month. Always deposit before the 5th of the month — ideally on April 1st or 2nd to maximize annual interest on the full ₹1.5L contribution.

PPF Extension — 5-Year Blocks After Maturity

After the 15-year lock-in, you have two options: Option 1 — Extend with contributions for 5 more years. Interest continues on new contributions plus existing corpus. Option 2 — Extend without contributions. The existing corpus continues to earn 7.1% tax-free interest without adding new money. Option 2 is often overlooked — it's essentially a tax-free fixed deposit that beats any bank FD rate. Many long-term investors hold PPF accounts for 25–30 years using silent extensions.

PPF Loan and Partial Withdrawal Rules

Loans against PPF: Available from the 3rd year to end of 6th year. Maximum loan = 25% of balance at end of 2nd preceding year. Interest on PPF loan = 1% above PPF rate (currently 8.1%). Partial withdrawals: Permitted from year 7 onward. Maximum 50% of balance at end of 4th year or end of preceding year (whichever is lower). Only one withdrawal per year allowed.

PPF vs NPS vs FD — Which Is Right for You?

PPF: 7.1% tax-free, government-backed, 15-year lock-in, EEE status, no market risk. Best for: conservative investors, those in 30% tax bracket (effective yield = 10.3%). NPS: 9–12% expected (market-linked), 60% corpus tax-free at retirement, partial lock-in, additional ₹50K deduction under 80CCD(1B). Best for: retirement planning supplement. Bank FD: 6.5–7.5% currently, fully taxable, flexible tenure. Best for: short-term goals, liquidity needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I open a PPF account for my minor child?
A: Yes — parents can open a PPF account in the name of a minor child. The combined limit of ₹1.5L applies to parent + child accounts together (not separately).

Q: What happens to PPF if I become an NRI?
A: NRIs cannot open new PPF accounts. If you become NRI after opening, the existing account can continue till maturity at 7.1% but cannot be extended beyond maturity. Post-maturity, the account earns only savings account interest.

Q: Is PPF interest compounded monthly or annually?
A: PPF interest is calculated monthly but credited annually on March 31st. Your account statement will show accumulated interest added to your balance at year-end.

CBSE Formula — The Most Common Conversion

For CBSE Class 10 and Class 12, the official conversion formula is: Percentage = CGPA × 9.5. This is CBSE's own formula, not a workaround. So CGPA 9.2 = 87.4%. CGPA 7.8 = 74.1%. CGPA 6.5 = 61.75%. This formula applies only to grades on the 10-point CGPA scale. Subject-specific GPA to percentage is calculated the same way (Subject grade × 9.5).

VTU (Visvesvaraya Technological University) Conversion

VTU (Bangalore) uses: Percentage = (CGPA − 0.75) × 10. So CGPA 8.5 = (8.5 − 0.75) × 10 = 77.5%. Note: This differs significantly from CBSE's formula. VTU 8.5 CGPA is NOT 85%. Always use the university-specific formula to avoid errors in job applications.

SPPU (Pune University) Conversion

Savitribai Phule Pune University (SPPU) uses: Percentage = CGPA × 10 − 7.5. This is equivalent to VTU's formula. CGPA 7.5 = 67.5%. CGPA 9.0 = 82.5%.

IIT/NIT Conversion (10-Point Scale)

Most IITs use a 10-point scale without a universal conversion formula to percentage. For recruitment, IIT CGPA is typically used as-is. Many companies accept 6.5+ CGPA from IITs as equivalent to a first class degree. For foreign university applications (MS/PhD), use CGPA directly on the 10-point scale — US universities understand it. For percentage conversion if needed: IIT/NIT typically uses CGPA × 9.5 (same as CBSE) or the university's specific conversion table.

SGPA vs CGPA — Clear Definitions

SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average) = Weighted average of grade points for one semester. Calculated as: Sum of (Subject Credits × Grade Point) ÷ Total Credits. CGPA (Cumulative GPA) = Weighted average of all SGPAs across all completed semesters. As you complete more semesters, each new SGPA updates your CGPA. Formula: CGPA = Sum of (SGPA × Semester Credits) ÷ Total Credits Completed.

Is Your CGPA Good Enough for Placements?

Company CGPA cutoffs vary significantly: Tier-1 IT (TCS, Infosys, Wipro): Minimum 6.0 CGPA across all semesters, no active backlogs. Tier-2 product companies: Usually 7.0+. MNCs and Big 4: 6.5–7.0+. FAANG/top product: 7.5+ with strong competitive programming profile. However, CGPA is a filter, not a selector — interviews, projects, and competitive achievements matter far more beyond the cutoff.

4.0 GPA Scale Conversion for US Applications

For MS/MBA applications in the USA, your Indian 10-point CGPA roughly maps as: 9.0–10.0 → 3.7–4.0 GPA. 8.0–8.9 → 3.3–3.7 GPA. 7.0–7.9 → 2.7–3.3 GPA. 6.0–6.9 → 2.0–2.7 GPA. Use WES (World Education Services) or official university evaluation for formal conversions. The WorldOfTools CGPA Calculator shows all these conversions side by side.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: My university says 7.5 CGPA = First Class. Is that accurate?

Q: How do I calculate CGPA if I have arrears?
A: Arrear grades (failed, reappeared, cleared) are included in CGPA calculation based on the higher mark upon clearing. Some universities recalculate CGPA after clearing while others retain the original grade — check your university's grade policy.

What Is a JWT — Structure Explained

A JWT consists of three parts separated by dots: header.payload.signature. Each part is Base64URL-encoded. Header: Contains token type ("JWT") and signing algorithm (HS256, RS256, etc.). Payload: Contains claims — registered (iss, exp, sub, aud), public, and private claims. This is where user ID, roles, permissions, and expiry live. Signature: HMAC or RSA-signed hash of header + payload. Only the server with the secret can generate or verify it.

Common JWT Claims — What Each Field Means

  • iss (Issuer): Who created the token — usually your auth server URL
  • sub (Subject): Who the token is about — usually user ID
  • aud (Audience): Who the token is intended for
  • exp (Expiration): Unix timestamp after which the token is invalid
  • iat (Issued At): Unix timestamp of when the token was created
  • nbf (Not Before): Token is not valid before this timestamp
  • jti (JWT ID): Unique identifier for the token (prevents replay attacks)

Security Warning — What NOT to Put in JWT Payloads

JWTs are encoded, not encrypted. Anyone with the token can decode the payload — base64 decoding is trivial. Never include: passwords or password hashes, credit card numbers, social security numbers, sensitive medical data, or any secret keys. The payload is meant for authorization claims (roles, permissions, user ID), not sensitive data storage. If you need encrypted tokens, use JWE (JSON Web Encryption), not JWT.

JWT vs Session Tokens — Architecture Comparison

Session tokens: Random string stored server-side. Server must look up the session DB on every request. Stateful — easy to invalidate. Better for monolithic apps. JWTs: Self-contained, verified by signature. No server DB lookup. Stateless — scales horizontally. Harder to invalidate before expiry. Better for distributed microservices and mobile APIs. How Exchange Rates Work — Mid-Market vs Bank Rate

The rate shown on WorldOfTools (and on Google) is the mid-market rate — the midpoint between the buy and sell rates in the interbank forex market. This is the "true" exchange rate. Banks and forex bureaus add a spread of 2–5% as profit, so: If mid-market USD/INR = 83.50, your bank may sell dollars at 86.00 and buy at 81.00. That 2–5% difference is pure profit for the bank — or savings for you if you find better rates.

TCS (Tax Collected at Source) on Foreign Exchange in India

From October 1, 2023, significant TCS rules apply to foreign currency transactions: Overseas tour packages: 20% TCS on full amount (no threshold). Foreign remittance (LRS): 20% TCS above ₹7 lakh/year (education loans: 0.5%, other education: 5%). Forex card / foreign currency purchase for travel: 20% TCS above ₹7 lakh. TCS is not a tax loss — it's advance tax you can claim back when filing ITR. However, it temporarily blocks working capital. Plan foreign currency purchases accordingly.

When INR weakens against USD (e.g., from ₹80 to ₹85): Importers pay more in rupees for same dollar-value goods, increasing costs. Exporters earn more rupees per dollar of revenue — beneficial.
