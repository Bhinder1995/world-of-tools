What is PPF (Public Provident Fund)?

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a government-backed, long-term savings scheme in India offering tax-free returns. Introduced in 1968, PPF follows the EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax model — contributions qualify for 80C deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh, interest earned is completely tax-free, and the maturity amount is also exempt from tax.

The current PPF interest rate is 7.1% per annum, compounded annually. The minimum tenure is 15 years, extendable in 5-year blocks. Deposits must be between ₹500 and ₹1,50,000 per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current PPF interest rate in 2026?

The current PPF interest rate is 7.1% per annum, compounded annually. This rate has been unchanged since April 2020 and is reviewed quarterly by the Government of India.

What is the minimum and maximum PPF deposit?

The minimum PPF deposit is ₹500 per year and the maximum is ₹1,50,000 per year. Deposits exceeding ₹1.5 lakh will not earn any additional interest on the excess amount.

Is PPF completely tax-free?

Yes. PPF follows the EEE model. Contributions qualify for Section 80C deduction (up to ₹1.5 lakh), interest is completely tax-free, and the maturity amount is also exempt from income tax.

Can I extend my PPF account after 15 years?

Yes. After the initial 15-year maturity, you can extend in 5-year blocks — either with continued deposits (to maximize returns) or without new deposits (corpus grows at 7.1% with no investment required).

When is PPF interest calculated?

PPF interest is calculated on the minimum balance between the 5th and last day of each month. To maximize interest, deposit your amount before the 5th of each month.

What happens if I miss a PPF deposit?

If you miss depositing at least ₹500 in any year, the account becomes discontinued. You must pay ₹50 per defaulted year as a penalty plus the minimum ₹500 deposit to revive it.
