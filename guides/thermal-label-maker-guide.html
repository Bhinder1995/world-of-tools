1. What is Thermal Printing?

Thermal printing is a digital printing process which produces a printed image by selectively heating coated thermochromic paper, or thermal paper as it is commonly known, when the paper passes over the thermal print head. The coating turns black in the areas where it is heated, producing an image.

Direct Thermal vs. Thermal Transfer

There are two primary types of thermal printing you should know about:

  • Direct Thermal: This is the most common type for shipping labels. It uses heat-sensitive paper and requires no ink or ribbon. It's cost-effective but sensitive to heat and sunlight over time.
  • Thermal Transfer: This uses a heated ribbon to produce long-lasting, durable images on a wide variety of materials. It's ideal for permanent assets or long-term inventory labels.
💡 Why use Thermal?

Thermal printers are the gold standard for e-commerce because they are incredibly fast, reliable, and require zero ink refills. If you're shipping more than 10 packages a week, a thermal printer will pay for itself in saved time and ink costs within months.

2. Creating 4x6 Shipping Labels

The 4x6 inch (101.6mm x 152.4mm) label is the universal standard for shipping. Major carriers like USPS, UPS, FedEx, and DHL all use this format.

Export to PDF

Always download your label as a **PDF**. Unlike images (PNG/JPG), PDF files contain vector information that keeps the text and barcodes crisp at any scale. Our tool automatically sets the PDF page size to exactly 4x6 inches.

Print Settings

Open the PDF and hit print. In your printer dialog, check three things:

  • Scale: Set to "Actual Size" or "100%". Never use "Fit to Page".
  • Orientation: Usually "Portrait" for 4x6.
  • Dithering: If available, set to "None" or "Halftone" for the sharpest text.

3. Barcode & QR Code Best Practices

A barcode is only useful if it's scannable. Poor quality thermal prints can make barcodes "fuzzy," leading to scanning failures at the post office.

Barcode Types Explained

  • Code 128: The standard for most logistics and shipping labels. It can store both numbers and letters.
  • EAN-13 / UPC-A: Used for retail products globally. These require specific number lengths (13 and 12 digits respectively).
  • QR Codes: High-density 2D codes that can store URLs or large amounts of text. Great for marketing labels.
⚠️ The "Quiet Zone" Rule

Always leave at least 0.25 inches (6mm) of white space around your barcode. This is called the "Quiet Zone." If text or borders are too close to the barcode, scanners may fail to recognize where the code begins and ends.
