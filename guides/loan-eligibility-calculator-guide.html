How Banks Calculate Your Eligibility

Banks use various factors to determine if you can afford a loan. The most critical one is your FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio). Most banks require that your total EMIs (including the new one) should not exceed 40–50% of your net monthly income.

Key Factors

  • Monthly Income: Higher income usually means higher eligibility.
  • Existing Liabilities: Other active loans reduce your borrowing power.
  • Age: Younger applicants get longer tenures, increasing eligibility.
  • Employment Type: Salaried employees often get slightly better terms than self-employed.

How to Use the Calculator

  1. Select Loan Type: Choose between Home, Personal, or Car Loan.
  2. Enter Monthly Income: Use your net (in-hand) salary after taxes.
  3. Add Existing EMIs: Include any current credit card, car, or personal loan payments.
  4. Set Interest Rate: Use the current market rate (e.g., 8.5% for Home Loans).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much home loan can I get on ₹50,000 salary?
On a ₹50,000 monthly salary with no existing EMIs, you can typically get a home loan of ₹35–40 lakhs for a 20-year tenure at 8.5% interest.
Does my credit score matter?
Yes! A high credit score (750+) can help you get a higher loan amount and potentially lower interest rates. This calculator assumes a standard profile.
