GMT vs. UTC: What's the Difference?

You'll often see time represented as "GMT+8" or "UTC-5." While they are often used interchangeably, they serve different purposes:

  • GMT (Greenwich Mean Time): A time zone centered on the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London. It is a historical and maritime standard.
  • UTC (Coordinated Universal Time): The modern scientific standard based on atomic clocks. UTC is not a time zone itself, but a "reference point" that all time zones are set against. For most practical purposes, they show the same time.

The Challenge of Daylight Saving Time (DST)

DST is the practice of advancing clocks during warmer months so that darkness falls at a later clock time. The problem? Not every country participates, and those that do change their clocks on different dates. For example, North America changes its clocks at a different time than Europe. This "Seasonal Drift" is the primary cause of missed meetings. Our tool accounts for current DST rules automatically.

🌍 Meeting Tip: The 24-Hour Rule

When scheduling international calls, always use the 24-hour clock (e.g., 14:00 instead of 2:00 PM) to avoid confusion between AM and PM. Using our converter to see the side-by-side comparison ensures that "9:00" in Tokyo doesn't turn into "9:00 PM" in London accidentally.
