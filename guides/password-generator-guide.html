What Makes a Password 'Secure'?

Security experts measure password strength using "Entropy"—a mathematical value that describes how unpredictable a string is. To maximize entropy, a password should include:

  • Length: 12 characters is the minimum, but 16+ is ideal for high-security accounts.
  • Complexity: A mix of Uppercase (A), Lowercase (a), Numbers (1), and Symbols (!@#).
  • Unpredictability: No words from a dictionary, no personal info, and no repeating patterns (like `abc` or `111`).

The Brute-Force Reality

A standard hacker toolkit can test billions of combinations per second. A 6-character password (lowercase only) is cracked instantly. A 12-character complex password would take an average desktop computer over 3,000 years to crack. The difference is life-changing.

🛡️ Security Rule: Never Reuse

The biggest risk isn't a hacker guessing your password; it's "Credential Stuffing." If a small website you used 5 years ago gets hacked, they will try that same email/password combination on Gmail, Facebook, and PayPal. Using a generator ensures EVERY account has a unique, unrelated key.
