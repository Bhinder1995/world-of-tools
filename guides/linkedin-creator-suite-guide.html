How Styled Text Works on Social Media

LinkedIn (and most social platforms) don't support HTML tags like `` or ``. Instead, our tool uses Unicode Mathematics Alphanumeric Symbols. These are separate characters in the Unicode standard that look like bold or italic letters. Because they are characters rather than formatting, they can be pasted into any text field and will retain their appearance.

  • Bold Serif/Sans: Perfect for headlines or announcing big news.
  • Italic/Script: Great for quotes, personal reflections, or professional disclaimers.
  • Monospace: Ideal for technical snippets or creating a "typewriter" aesthetic.

Post Structure for Maximum Engagement

A great LinkedIn post follows a specific rhythm:

  • The Body: Use white space! Paragraphs should be no more than 2-3 lines long. Use bullet points to make your content skimmable.

💡 Algorithm Insight: The Dwell Time

LinkedIn tracks "Dwell Time"—how long a user spends looking at your post. If you use interesting formatting and clean spacing, users stay longer, which signals to the algorithm that your content is high-quality.

How to Use the LinkedIn Creator Suite

  1. Draft Your Post: Type your message directly into the editor or paste it from your notes.
  2. Select and Style: Highlight the specific words or phrases you want to emphasize and click the style buttons (Bold, Italic, etc.).
  3. Check Character Limits: LinkedIn has a 3,000-character limit for posts. Our tool keeps track of this for you.
  4. Copy and Paste: Once your post looks perfect, hit copy and paste it directly into the LinkedIn "Start a post" box.

Common Pitfalls: Accessibility and Screen Readers

  • Don't Overdo It: Unicode characters can be difficult for screen readers to translate. Use styling for emphasis, not for your entire post.
  • Check for Mobile: Some older android devices might not render all Unicode styles correctly. Stick to the "Serif" or "Sans" Bold options for the best cross-device compatibility.
  • Avoid External Links in the Post: The current LinkedIn algorithm often penalizes posts that lead users off the platform. Add your link in the "First Comment" instead!
