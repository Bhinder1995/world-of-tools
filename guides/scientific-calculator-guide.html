Key Functions Explained

A scientific calculator can look intimidating with its array of buttons. Here is a breakdown of the most critical zones:

  • Trigonometry (SIN, COS, TAN): Used to calculate the ratios of sides in a right-angled triangle. These are essential for architecture, navigation, and wave physics.
  • Logarithms (LOG, LN): These functions help solve equations where an unknown variable is an exponent. They are used extensively in measuring sound intensity (decibels), earthquake magnitude (Richter scale), and pH levels.
  • Exponentials & Roots (x², x^y, √): Crucial for calculating compound interest, population growth, and geometric scaling.
  • Degree vs. Radian Mode: One of the most common sources of error. Degrees slice a circle into 360 parts, while Radians use the radius (approx 57.3 degrees). Always check your mode before performing trig functions!

📐 Radian Alert!

In calculus and higher-level physics, Radians are the standard. If your sin(90) isn't returning 1 , you're likely in Radian mode (where sin(90) is roughly 0.89). Toggle the mode button to fix this.
