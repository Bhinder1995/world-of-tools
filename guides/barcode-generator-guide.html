One-Dimensional (1D) vs. Two-Dimensional (2D) Codes

While we also offer a QR (2D) generator, a traditional barcode (1D) is still the standard for many retail and logistics operations.

  • 1D Barcodes: These store data in the varying widths and spacings of parallel lines. Examples include Code 128, EAN, and UPC. They are generally read by laser scanners.
  • 2D Barcodes (QR): These store data in squares and dots, allowing for more information (like URLs) but require camera-based scanners or smartphones.

Key Barcode Formats Supported

  • Code 128: The most versatile 1D format. It can encode both numbers and letters, making it the choice for internal inventory and non-retail tracking.
  • EAN-13: The global standard for retail products outside of North America. It consists of 13 digits and is essential if you plan to sell in physical stores worldwide.
  • UPC (Universal Product Code): The retail standard in the USA and Canada. It primarily uses a 12-digit format.
  • ITF-14: Often used on shipping cartons and boxes to identify the contents inside.

📏 Printing Tip: Quiet Zones

Every barcode needs a "Quiet Zone"—a white, empty space to the left and right of the bars. If you print your barcode too close to an edge or other text, scanners will fail to identify where the barcode begins and ends. Always leave at least 1/4 inch of clear space around your code.
