What is OCR and Why Do You Need It?

OCR (Optical Character Recognition) is a technology that allows computers to "read" text inside images. Whether you have a photo of a textbook page, a physical receipt, or a screenshot of a website—OCR can convert the pixels into editable text strings.

Our **Image to Text tool** is powered by Tesseract.js, allowing high-performance OCR to happen directly in your browser without uploading your sensitive documents to any server. This is essential for students, researchers, and office professionals.

Step-by-Step Guide to Convert Photo to Text

  1. Choose Language: Select the primary language of your image (English, Hindi, etc.). Higher accuracy is achieved by selecting just one main language.
  2. Upload Image: Select your JPEG, PNG, or WebP file. For best results, ensure the image is high resolution and not blurry.
  3. Automatic Scanning: Our tool will initialize the OCR worker and start "reading" the image. You can watch the progress bar in real-time.
  4. Edit & Export: Once finished, the text appears in a box. You can manually edit it before using the "Copy" or "Download .txt" buttons.

Pro Tips for High Accuracy OCR

  • Clean Contrast: Ensure the background of your image is clean. Dark text on a light background is easiest for the AI engine to read.
  • Avoid Skewing: Try to take photos of documents from directly above to prevent distorted text orientation.
  • Language Sensitivity: If your image has mixed languages, English and Hindi for example, English usually performs best as the primary language.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a limit to the image size?

We recommend images under 15MB for optimal browser performance. Very large images might crash the WASM worker if your device has low RAM.

Will it work for handwritten notes?

Our tool works best for printed text (documents, screenshots). While it can read clear handwriting, its accuracy is significantly lower compared to printed fonts.

Can I extract text from a PDF?

Currently, this tool is optimized for images (JPG, PNG). For PDFs, we recommend converting to JPG first or using a dedicated PDF text extractor tool.
